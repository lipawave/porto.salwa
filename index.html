How I work

What I took from there.

  1. Understand the flow first, then do the work.

    Before writing a letter, I need to know who needs it, when, and where it will be filed.

    From HIMSIKA

  2. If the other person doesn't get it, it isn't done.

    A progress report that's correct but confuses parents still hasn't done its job.

    From Timedoor

  3. Anything done over and over deserves a system.

    That's why I built the correspondence automation below.

    From HIMSIKA

  4. Write it down, so nothing depends on memory.

    Minutes and archives aren't a formality. They're how an organization remembers.

    From Education Fair and HIMSIKA

Case study · HIMSIKA

Behind every letter that went out on time.

As Vice General Secretary, I saw the same steps repeat for every letter. So I built a small system to carry them.

What I did

Mapped the steps a letter goes through, then connected Google Forms, Sheets, Drive, and Apps Script so a request turns into a filed document.

How I tested it

Ran it with real requests before calling it ready, and fixed what broke along the way.

Where it stands

It works, but it isn't officially adopted by the organization yet. Next, I want the flow documented well enough that the next secretary can run it without me.

Case study · Education Fair 2026

Big event, a lot of paper.

As Executive Secretary, I handled the administration side of the event: official letters to partners, participant certificates, documentation, and the accountability report afterward.

Much of it was a team effort. What I own is keeping the documents consistent and making sure each one reached the right person at the right step.
